# Global Life Expectancy Analysis

![Dashboard](Images/Distribution%20of%20developed%20and%20developing%20countries.png)


## Project Overview

This project investigates factors influencing life expectancy differences between developed and developing countries from 2000 to 2015.

The analysis uses WHO Global Health Observatory data to explore relationships between life expectancy, healthcare, immunization, economic conditions and lifestyle factors.


## Research Questions

This project aims to answer:

1. How does immunization coverage impact life expectancy?

2. What relationships exist between life expectancy and lifestyle factors such as BMI and alcohol consumption?


## Dataset

The dataset contains:

- 193 countries
- 22 variables
- Time period: 2000 - 2015

Variables include:

- Life expectancy
- Immunization indicators
- Healthcare expenditure
- GDP
- Income composition
- BMI
- Alcohol consumption

# Key Insights


## 1. Healthcare & Immunization

Immunization coverage demonstrates a positive relationship with life expectancy.

Developed countries generally show stronger vaccination programs, while improving immunization coverage is particularly valuable for countries with weaker healthcare systems.


![Healthcare and Immunization](Images/Life%20expectancy%20by%20expenditure%20and%20immunization.png)



## 2. Economic Factors

Economic conditions and income composition show strong positive relationships with life expectancy.

Countries with higher socioeconomic development tend to achieve higher life expectancy levels.


![Economic Factors](Images/Other%20factors%20on%20life%20expectancy.png)



## 3. Lifestyle Factors

Lifestyle indicators such as BMI and alcohol consumption show weaker or mixed relationships with life expectancy.

These relationships may reflect broader development factors rather than direct impacts.

# Data Limitations

- Imbalanced sample size between developed and developing countries
- Missing data may affect accuracy of averages and comparisons


# Conclusion

Higher immunization coverage, healthcare access and socioeconomic conditions are strongly associated with higher life expectancy.

Lifestyle factors show more complex relationships and should be interpreted together with broader development indicators.
